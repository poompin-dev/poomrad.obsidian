## Epidemiology
- 2 age peaks
	- 3rd decades
	- Elderly (>50 years)
- Slight men predominance (except NS subtype which has equal gender predilection)
## Clinical and diagnosis
- Cervical chain lymph nodes enlargement.
- **Contiguous** nodal involvement and spread are characteristics.
	- Infradiaphragmatic disease (≥ stage III) is more likely in the presence of supraclavicular or bilateral neck adenopathy.
	- Exclusive infradiaphragmatic nodal disease is rare (<10%)
- B symptoms are more frequently present than [[32_Multisystem/Neoplasm/Haematologic malignancy/Lymphoma/Non-Hodgkin Lymphoma\|NHL]].
- Tissue biopsy is need for diagnosis.
	- Surgical excision of the entire node is more preferred than CNB for evaluation of LN architecture.
- Specific laboratory investigations
	- ESR
	- LDH
## Staging
Currently using [[32_Multisystem/Neoplasm/Haematologic malignancy/Lymphoma/Lugano staging classification (2014)\|Lugano staging classification (2014)]]
- Key distinction is **limited** vs **advanced** stage disease
- Supercede [[32_Multisystem/Neoplasm/Haematologic malignancy/Lymphoma/Ann Arbor staging classification\|Ann Arbor staging classification]]
- No longer use suffix "X" as "bulky" disease definition depends on lymphoma subtype
### Caveats
- Splenomegaly and BM uptake on FDG PET/CT may be due to reactive process rather than disease involvement.
- If no evidence of BM uptake on FDG PET/CT → BM biopsy is *no* longer required.
## Prognosis
Factors that associated with poorer prognosis includes:
- Older patients.
- MC and LD CHL subtypes.
- Raised ESR
- B symptoms
- Multiple sites of disease
- Bulky mediastinal disease (> 10 cm at CT)
The last two factors are determined by imaging which signify importance of radiologist to always document **site** and **size** of the disease.
## Treatment
- Curative aim with great success (>90% 5-year survival rates with early-stage disease)
- "Mantle" radiotherapy was commonly utilised in the past due to high radiosensitivity of HL
	- The radiotherapy field includes cervical nodes, axillae, and mediastinum down to level of T10
	- Now RT is more selectively used due to awareness of harm due to:
		- Secondary cancers: thyroid, breast, etc.
		- Coronary artery disease.
- Interim FDG PET/CT imaging (iPET), done after two cycles of CMT, allow escalation or de-escalation of therapy in early-stage disease.

## Histopathology
- Defined by the "**Reed-Sternberg cell**", a large binucleated blast cell.
- 2 distinct entities are:
	- **Classical HL** (CHL, 95%)
	- **Nodular lymphocyte-predominant HL** (NLPHL)

### Classical HL
CHL is subdivided into 4 histological types, all share the same immunophenotype. By descending in prevalence in Western population:
- Nodular sclerosing (NS)
	- Most common subtypes
	- Typically present with **bulky** anterior mediastinal mass.
- Mixed cellularity (MC)
	- Associated with [[32_Multisystem/Infectious/Epstein-Barr virus\|EBV]] (patients who have had infectious mononucleosis)
- Lymphocyte-rich
- Lymphocyte-depleted (LD)

**MC HL and LD HL:**
- are more common in developing countries.
- are associated with HIV infection.
- have more aggressive clinical course.

## Nodular lymphocyte-predominant HL
- Tends to occur in middle-aged men.
- Clinically indolent with excellent overall survival.
