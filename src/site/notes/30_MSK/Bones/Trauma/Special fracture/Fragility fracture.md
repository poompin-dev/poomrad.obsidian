> [!summary]
> A fracture caused by low-energy trauma that would not ordinarily fracture healthy bone, conventionally a fall from standing height or less, and therefore a sentinel event for impaired bone strength and further fracture risk.

---
## Overview
- The diagnosis depends on the **mechanism and clinical context**, not a specific fracture morphology.
- Most are related to [[30_MSK/Bones/Metabolic/Osteoporosis/Osteoporosis\|osteoporosis]], but a fragility fracture can occur without a DXA T-score ≤ −2.5 because bone strength also depends on microarchitecture, geometry and quality.
- Common sites are the **vertebrae, proximal femur, distal radius and proximal humerus**; the pelvis, ribs and proximal tibia are also recognised sites.

---
## Imaging approach

### Radiograph
- First-line examination for most symptomatic sites.
- May miss nondisplaced proximal femoral, pelvic, sacral and early insufficiency fractures.

### CT
- Defines cortical disruption, displacement, comminution, articular extension and complications.
- Useful after negative or indeterminate radiographs when an occult hip fracture remains suspected, and for excluding an underlying destructive lesion.

### MR
- Most sensitive for a radiographically occult fracture, showing the fracture line and associated marrow oedema.
- For persistent clinical suspicion of hip fracture after negative radiographs, **MRI without contrast or CT without contrast** is appropriate.
