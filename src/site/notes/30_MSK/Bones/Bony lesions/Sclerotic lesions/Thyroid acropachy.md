> [!summary]
> Rare, late manifestation of **autoimmune thyroid disease**, usually severe Graves disease, characterised by **digital clubbing, acral soft-tissue swelling, and irregular periosteal new bone formation in the hands and feet**. It is an important differential diagnosis of [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|hypertrophic osteoarthropathy]].

---

## Clinical context
- Rarest major extrathyroidal manifestation of Graves disease (**<1%**)
- Usually occurs in patients with:
	- Severe **thyroid ophthalmopathy**
	- **Thyroid dermopathy / pretibial myxoedema**
- May develop after treatment of thyrotoxicosis while the patient is **euthyroid or hypothyroid**.
- Isolated acropachy without ophthalmopathy or dermopathy is unusual.

---

## Pathophysiology
- Exact mechanism is uncertain.
- Autoimmune activation of periosteal and soft-tissue fibroblasts is thought to produce:
	- Fibroblast proliferation
	- Glycosaminoglycan deposition
	- Periosteal new bone formation
- TSH-receptor and IGF-1-receptor autoantibodies may contribute.

---

## Clinical presentation
- **Digital clubbing**
- Fusiform swelling and tightness of the fingers and toes
- Soft-tissue oedema of the hands and feet
- Usually painless or mildly symptomatic
	- Painful periostitis and impaired digit function can occur in severe disease.
- Look for the accompanying Graves phenotype:
	- Proptosis / thyroid ophthalmopathy
	- Pretibial myxoedema or other thyroid dermopathy

---

## Imaging

### Radiograph
- Best demonstrated in the **hands and feet**
- Irregular periosteal new bone along the:
	- Metacarpals
	- Metatarsals
	- Proximal and middle phalanges
- Characteristic morphology:
	- **Spiculated**
	- **Frothy / fluffy**
	- **Feathery or lacy**
- Usually affects the mid-diaphyseal periosteum with adjacent soft-tissue swelling.
- Periostitis may be bilateral but **asymmetric or uneven**.
- Long-bone involvement is uncommon, although the radius, ulna, tibia, or fibula may occasionally be affected.

> [!tip]
> The combination of **acral distribution + frothy/spiculated periostitis + Graves ophthalmopathy and dermopathy** strongly favours thyroid acropachy over HOA.

### Bone scintigraphy
- May show focal increased uptake at involved periosteal sites.
- Unlike HOA, a diffuse symmetric long-bone **[[38_Glossary/Double stripe sign\|double-stripe / tramline pattern]]** is not typical.

---
## Management
- There is no established specific treatment supported by high-quality evidence.
- Coordinate treatment of Graves disease and its ophthalmic and dermal manifestations.
- Symptomatic analgesia may be used when periostitis is painful.
