> [!summary]
> **Clinical–radiologic syndrome** characterised by **digital clubbing, periostosis of tubular bones, and arthralgia / joint effusions**. The syndrome may be incomplete. First classify true HOA as **primary or secondary**, then distinguish it from close mimics—especially [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Thyroid acropachy\|thyroid acropachy]].

---

## Classification

### True hypertrophic osteoarthropathy

- [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|Primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy]]
	- Hereditary form (**pachydermoperiostosis**)
	- Usually begins around puberty
- [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Secondary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|Secondary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy]]
	- Acquired form caused by an underlying disease
	- **Hypertrophic pulmonary osteoarthropathy (HPOA)** is the pulmonary / intrathoracic subset

### Closest mimic

- [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Thyroid acropachy\|Thyroid acropachy]]
	- Autoimmune manifestation of Graves disease
	- Produces clubbing, acral swelling, and periosteal new bone but is **not an HOA subtype**

---

## Shared clinical pattern

- Digital clubbing of the fingers and toes
- Enlargement and swelling of the distal extremities
- Periostosis of tubular bones
- Arthralgia, arthritis, or joint effusions
- Bone pain and limb tenderness, particularly in secondary HOA
- Pachydermia may occur in primary HOA but is not required for the overall syndrome.

---

## Shared imaging pattern

### Radiograph

- Bilateral periosteal new bone formation involving multiple tubular bones
- Long bones of the distal extremities are commonly affected:
	- Radius and ulna
	- Tibia and fibula
- Morphology ranges from thin and smooth to lamellated, solid, or irregular according to the cause and disease duration.
- Associated soft-tissue swelling and digital clubbing
- Absence of a destructive medullary lesion supports a systemic periostosis rather than multifocal neoplasm or infection.

### Bone scintigraphy

- Sensitive for early or radiographically subtle periostosis
- Symmetric linear cortical uptake along the long bones
- Parallel uptake along both cortices produces the **[[38_Glossary/Double stripe sign\|double-stripe / tramline sign]]**
- The sign supports HOA but is not entirely specific.

## Primary HOA vs secondary HOA vs thyroid acropachy

| Feature                   | **[[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|Primary HOA]]** | **[[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Secondary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|Secondary HOA]]**                          | **[[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Thyroid acropachy\|Thyroid acropachy]]**                                          |
| ------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Classification**        | True HOA                                                   | True HOA                                                                                | HOA mimic; not a subtype                                           |
| **Typical setting**       | Hereditary; onset around puberty                           | Acquired; usually adult; underlying systemic disease                                    | Long-standing autoimmune thyroid disease, usually Graves disease   |
| **Distribution**          | Diffuse long and short tubular bones                       | Bilateral long bones; diaphyseal → metaphyseal                                          | Hands and feet; metacarpals, metatarsals, and phalanges            |
| **Periosteal morphology** | Exuberant, irregular, or shaggy                            | Usually smooth, solid, or lamellated                                                    | Irregular, frothy, spiculated, or lacy                             |
| **Epiphyses**             | May be involved                                            | Typically spared                                                                        | Acral distribution is more useful than epiphyseal involvement      |
| **Symmetry**              | Usually bilateral and symmetric                            | Usually bilateral and symmetric                                                         | May be bilateral but uneven or asymmetric                          |
| **Clinical clues**        | Pachydermia, cutis verticis gyrata, hyperhidrosis          | Clubbing with prominent bone pain or arthralgia; pulmonary disease or malignancy common | Graves ophthalmopathy and thyroid dermopathy / pretibial myxoedema |
| **Decisive action**       | Exclude a secondary cause ± genetic evaluation             | Find and treat the underlying cause                                                     | Confirm the Graves phenotype; reconsider HOA if it is absent       |

## Practical diagnostic approach
1. Confirm **true clubbing and multifocal periosteal new bone formation**.
2. Assess the pattern:
	- **Acral + frothy / spiculated** → consider [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Thyroid acropachy\|thyroid acropachy]]
	- **Long-bone + smooth / lamellated** → favours [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Secondary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|secondary HOA]]
	- **Exuberant / shaggy + epiphyseal extension** → favours [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|primary HOA]]
3. Use the clinical context:
	- Graves ophthalmopathy and dermopathy → thyroid acropachy
	- Childhood / adolescent onset with pachydermia → primary HOA
	- New adult-onset clubbing, bone pain, or periostosis → secondary HOA
4. In a new adult presentation, **search for a secondary cause—especially intrathoracic malignancy—before diagnosing primary HOA**.

---
## Other causes of multifocal periostosis
- Chronic venous insufficiency
- Voriconazole-induced periostitis
- Hypervitaminosis A / retinoid therapy
- Prostaglandin E therapy in infants
- Psoriatic arthritis
- Multifocal infection or neoplasm
- Renal osteodystrophy
	- **Subperiosteal resorption** may mimic periosteal new bone formation.
