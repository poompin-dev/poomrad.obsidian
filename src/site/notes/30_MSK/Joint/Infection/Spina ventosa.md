> [!summary]
> Characteristic form of paediatric tuberculous dactylitis involving a short tubular bone of the hand or foot, caused by haematogenous medullary infection and granulomatous tissue expansion.

Classically occurs in children younger than 5 years and presents as chronic, often painless fusiform swelling. The hand is affected more often than the foot.

---
## Appearance

### Radiograph
Fusiform or ballooned expansion of a metacarpal, metatarsal or phalanx with internal cyst-like lucencies, trabecular loss, cortical thinning and soft tissue swelling.

- Early: soft tissue swelling with or without periostitis.
- Established: expansile osteolysis with variable marginal sclerosis and periosteal thickening.
- Advanced: cortical breach, sinus or cold abscess, and possible adjacent joint extension. A sequestrum may occur but is not required.

### MRI
- Defines marrow replacement, enhancing granulation tissue, cortical breach, abscess, and tendon-sheath or joint extension.

> [!warning]
> Imaging is suggestive rather than diagnostic: obtain aspirate or biopsy for histology, acid-fast bacilli smear, nucleic acid amplification, mycobacterial culture and drug-susceptibility testing.

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity                                                          | Key differences                                                                                                        |
| --------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Congenital syphilis                                             | Infancy, usually bilateral or multidigit disease with symmetrical periostitis and metaphyseal abnormalities elsewhere. |
| Sickle cell dactylitis                                          | Acute painful swelling in an infant or toddler with a haemoglobinopathy; commonly bilateral or multidigit.             |
| Pyogenic, fungal or non-tuberculous mycobacterial osteomyelitis | Imaging overlap is substantial; pyogenic infection is usually more acute and inflammatory.                             |
| [[30_MSK/Bones/Bony lesions/Lytic lesions/Benign/Enchondroma\|Enchondroma]]                                                 | Central expansile lucency without inflammatory soft tissue disease, sinus or abscess unless complicated by fracture.   |
| Sarcoid dactylitis                                              | Usually lacy or lattice-like phalangeal osteolysis in established multisystem sarcoidosis.                             |
