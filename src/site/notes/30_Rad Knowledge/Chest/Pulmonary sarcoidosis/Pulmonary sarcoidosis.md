## Overview
A multisystem inflammatory disease of unknown aetiology characterised by the presence of **non-caseating granulomata**.
* **Pathophysiology:** T-cell mediated response to unidentified antigens.
* **Key Lab Finding:** Bronchoalveolar lavage (BAL) shows **lymphocytosis** with a high **CD4:CD8 ratio** (>3.5 is highly specific).
* **Epidemiology:** Bimodal age peak (20–29 and >50). Slight female predominance.

---
## Clinical presentation
* **Asymptomatic:** ~50% discovered incidentally on CXR.
* **Respiratory:** Dry cough, dyspnoea, chest pain.
* **Systemic:** Fatigue, weight loss, fever.
* **Specific Syndromes:**
    * [[30_Rad Knowledge/Chest/Pulmonary sarcoidosis/Löfgren syndrome\|Löfgren syndrome]]: Acute presentation. Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy + Erythema nodosum + Arthralgia. Good prognosis (>90% resolution).
    * Heerfordt syndrome (Uveoparotid fever): Parotitis + Uveitis + Facial nerve palsy.
    * Lupus pernio: Chronic violaceous skin lesions (nose/cheeks). Poor prognosis (associated with chronic fibrosis).

## Imaging findings

### Chest radiograph (CXR)
The primary staging tool. See [[30_Rad Knowledge/Chest/Pulmonary sarcoidosis/Scadding staging\|Scadding staging]].

| Stage | Radiographic findings | Spontaneous resolution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **0** | Normal radiograph (extranodal disease) | N/A |
| **I** | **[[Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy\|Bilateral hilar lymphadenopathy]] (BHL)** ± right paratracheal nodes | 60–80% |
| **II** | BHL + Pulmonary infiltrates | 50–60% |
| **III** | Pulmonary infiltrates **without** adenopathy | <30% |
| **IV** | Pulmonary fibrosis (upper lobe volume loss) | 0% |

> [!tip] Exam Pearl: The "1-2-3 Sign" (Garland Triad)
> Symmetric enlargement of:
> 1. Right paratracheal nodes
> 2. Right hilar nodes
> 3. Left hilar nodes
>
> *Differentiation:* The **aortic-pulmonary (AP) window** is typically preserved in sarcoidosis, whereas it is often obscured in [[30_Rad Knowledge/Multisystem/Neoplasms/Lymphoma/Lymphoma\|lymphoma]].

### CT Chest (HRCT)
**Dominant Pattern:** Perilymphatic distribution with upper and middle zone predominance.
* **Nodal Disease:**
    * BHL and right paratracheal nodes (classic).
    * Calcification: rare early; late "eggshell" (ddx [[Silicosis\|Silicosis]]) or amorphous "icing sugar".
    * *Note:* In the Thai context, necrotic nodes suggest [[Tuberculosis\|Tuberculosis]] until proven otherwise. Sarcoid nodes are generally non-necrotic on CT.

* **Parenchymal Disease:**
    * **Micronodules (2–4mm):** Well-defined, irregular margins.
    * **Distribution:** Along bronchovascular bundles (beading), interlobular septa, and subpleural surfaces (fissures).
    * **Galaxy sign:** Confluence of micronodules into a central mass with peripheral satellite nodules. 
    * **Reversibility:** Ground-glass opacification (alveolitis) suggests active disease; potentially reversible with steroids.

* **Fibrotic Disease (Stage IV):**
    * **Posterior displacement of the main bronchi:** A hallmark of sarcoid fibrosis due to posterior upper lobe volume loss.
    * Traction bronchiectasis.
    * Architectural distortion.
    * *Note:* Honeycombing is rare (unlike UIP).

### Nuclear Medicine
* **Gallium-67 Citrate:**
    * **Lambda sign (λ):** Increased uptake in paratracheal and hilar nodes.
    * **Panda sign:** Increased uptake in lacrimal and parotid glands.
* **FDG-PET/CT:** Useful for assessing active inflammation (cardiac sarcoid or active alveolitis) when CT is equivocal.

## Differential diagnosis
### Bilateral Hilar Lymphadenopathy (BHL)
* **[[Tuberculosis\|Tuberculosis]] (TB):** Critical ddx in Thailand. TB is usually unilateral/asymmetric. If bilateral, look for necrosis (hypodense centre) or cavitary lung lesions.
* **[[30_Rad Knowledge/Multisystem/Neoplasms/Lymphoma/Lymphoma\|Lymphoma]]:** Often asymmetric, massive mediastinal involvement, anterior mediastinal mass (Hodgkin's), retrosternal space obliteration.
* **[[Silicosis\|Silicosis]] / CWP:** Occupational history is key. Eggshell calcification is more common in silicosis than sarcoid.
* **Metastases:** Renal cell carcinoma (RCC), Testicular seminoma, Melanoma.
### Perilymphatic Nodules
* **[[Lymphangitis carcinomatosis\|Lymphangitis carcinomatosis]]:** Septal thickening is usually **smooth** (sarcoid is nodular/beaded). Predominantly lower zone and asymmetric/unilateral. Known malignancy.
* **[[Pneumoconiosis\|Pneumoconiosis]]:** Silicosis (upper zone predominant).
* **Amyloidosis:** Rare.

> [!warning] Pitfall: Stage vs Severity
> [[30_Rad Knowledge/Chest/Pulmonary sarcoidosis/Scadding staging\|Scadding staging]] does **not** correlate with the severity of symptoms or PFT impairment. Stage IV (burnt-out fibrosis) may be stable, while Stage II may represent severe active alveolitis requiring aggressive immunosuppression.

> [!note] Thai Board Relevance
> In Thailand, distinguishing Sarcoidosis from **Tuberculosis** and **Fungal infections** (e.g., *Penicillium marneffei* / Talaromycosis in immunocompromised) is the primary exam challenge. Always check for necrosis in lymph nodes; if present, Sarcoid is unlikely.
