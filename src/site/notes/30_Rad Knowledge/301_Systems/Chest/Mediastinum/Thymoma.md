> [!info] Key points
> - Most common primary anterior mediastinal neoplasm in adults; peak 40-60 yrs, no clear sex predilection.
> - 30-50% have myasthenia gravis; conversely ~10-15% of MG patients harbour a thymoma.
> - Masaoka-Koga stage (capsular integrity, invasion, nodes, metastases) drives prognosis and management.
> - Pleural "drop" metastases are the classic pattern of spread; haematogenous spread is uncommon.
> - Low/moderate FDG avidity is typical; intense uptake favours **thymic carcinoma**.

---
## Epidemiology
- Adults 40-60 yrs; rare under 20.
- No strong environmental risk factors.
- Strong association with autoimmune disease, particularly myasthenia gravis.

## Pathophysiology
Neoplasm of thymic epithelial cells with variable admixed non-neoplastic lymphocytes. 

> WHO classifies by epithelial cell morphology and lymphocyte content:
>- **Type A**: spindle/oval epithelial cells, few lymphocytes
>- **Type AB**: mixed
>- **Type B1-B3**: increasing epithelial component, decreasing lymphocytes; B3 borders on thymic carcinoma
>- **Type C**: [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Chest/Mediastinum/Thymic carcinoma\|thymic carcinoma]] (distinct entity)

Behaviour ranges from indolent encapsulated lesion to locally invasive tumour with transcapsular, pericardial, pleural and (rarely) haematogenous spread.

## Clinical features
- Often incidental on imaging.
- Local: chest pain, cough, SVC obstruction (large/invasive).
- Paraneoplastic / autoimmune:
    - **[[31_Medical knowledge/Misc/Paraneoplastic syndromes/Myasthenia gravis\|Myasthenia gravis]]** (commonest)
    - **Pure red cell aplasia**
    - **Hypogammaglobulinaemia (Good syndrome)**
    - SLE, polymyositis, others less common

## Imaging

### CXR
- Anterior mediastinal mass or mediastinal widening; loss of retrosternal clear space on lateral.
- May be subtle if small.
- Raised hemidiaphragm: phrenic nerve palsy from invasion.

### CT (modality of choice)
- Well-defined, round or lobulated anterior mediastinal soft tissue mass, usually unilateral and off-midline.
- Homogeneous when small; heterogeneous with cystic change, haemorrhage or necrosis when larger.
- Calcification: capsular or coarse internal; non-specific, neither confirms nor excludes invasion.
- Mild to moderate enhancement.

>Masaoka-Koga staging
> - I capsulated
> - II microscopic/macroscopic transcapsular
> - III adjacent organ invasion
> - IVA pleural/pericardial
> - IVB lymphogenous/haematogenous.

>[!tip] **Features suspicious for invasion (Masaoka-Koga upstaging):**
> - Irregular or lobulated margins with loss of mediastinal fat planes (suggestive, not definitive)
> - Pericardial thickening, effusion, or direct contact with pericardial deformity
> - Great vessel encasement or clear infiltration
> - Pleural nodules / thickening (drop metastases) or pleural effusion
> - Phrenic nerve palsy on CXR/CT

### MRI
- Problem-solving for local invasion (pericardium, great vessels) when CT equivocal.
- **[[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Chest/Mediastinum/Thymus chemical shift MRI\|Chemical shift imaging]]**: thymoma shows **no signal drop** on opposed-phase, whereas **thymic hyperplasia/rebound drops signal** (intermixed fat). Useful in young patients post-chemotherapy or steroid withdrawal.

### FDG PET/CT
- Uptake variable; generally **low to moderate SUVmax**.
- Intense FDG avidity should raise suspicion of **[[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Chest/Mediastinum/Thymic carcinoma\|thymic carcinoma]]** or [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Multisystem/Neoplasm/Hematologic malignancy/Lymphoma/Lymphoma signpost\|lymphoma]] rather than indolent thymoma.
- More useful for detecting pleural/extrathoracic disease than for grading.

## Management
- **Surgery**: complete thymectomy is mainstay for resectable disease (Masaoka-Koga I-II and selected III).
- **Adjuvant RT**: for incomplete resection, capsular invasion, or stage III-IVA.
- **Chemotherapy** (platinum-based): unresectable, metastatic, or as neoadjuvant for borderline-resectable invasive disease.
- MG: thymectomy can improve symptoms; perioperative optimisation with neurology essential.
- Long follow-up: late recurrence is well recognised (up to 10-15 yrs).

---

> [!tip] Exam pearls
> - **Low FDG = thymoma; high FDG = thymic carcinoma or lymphoma.**
> - **Chemical shift MRI**: drop in signal on opposed phase = hyperplasia, not thymoma.
> - **Pleural drop metastases** are the hallmark of invasive thymoma; haematogenous spread is rare.
> - **Masaoka-Koga staging** (not WHO) drives management decisions.

> [!note] trivial
> Zebra Osseous metaplasia within thymoma is very rare; coarse internal calcification can mimic teratoma.
