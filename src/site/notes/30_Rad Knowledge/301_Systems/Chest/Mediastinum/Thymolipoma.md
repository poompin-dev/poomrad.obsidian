> [!info] Key points
> - **Benign thymic neoplasm composed of mature fat + scattered non-neoplastic thymic tissue.**
> - Can grow very large and **drape over the heart** ("drape sign"), mimicking **cardiomegaly** on CXR.
> - **Fat attenuation mass with internal soft tissue strands** on CT is essentially pathognomonic.
> - No malignant potential; surgical resection is curative.
 
---
## Epidemiology & clinical
- Rare: ~2-9% of thymic tumours.
- Any age, often diagnosed in young to middle-aged adults; no sex predilection.
- Frequently **asymptomatic** and incidental.
- Symptoms when giant: dyspnoea, cough, chest discomfort from mass effect.
- Can reach very large size (>20-30 cm reported) without invading adjacent structures, owing to pliability.
- Association with **[[31_Medical knowledge/Misc/Paraneoplastic syndromes/Myasthenia gravis\|Myasthenia gravis]]** is reported, though far less consistent than with [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Chest/Mediastinum/Thymoma\|thymoma]].
## Pathohistology
Mixed lesion of **mature adipose tissue (predominant)** and **non-neoplastic thymic epithelial and lymphoid elements**.
 
---
## Imaging
### CXR
- Apparent **cardiomegaly** or smooth anterior mediastinal opacity contiguous with the cardiac silhouette.
- "**Slump**" or **drape sign**: soft, pliable mass settles inferiorly and conforms to the heart and diaphragm; shape changes with positioning.
- May mimic pericardial fat pad, lipoma, or anterior diaphragmatic hernia.
### CT (most helpful)
- **Predominantly fat-attenuation** (-50 to -150 HU) anterior mediastinal mass.
- **Internal whorls, strands, or nodules of soft tissue** representing residual thymic tissue, often arranged linearly.
- **Well-defined**, non-infiltrative; no encasement or invasion of adjacent structures.
- Contiguous with the expected position of the thymus.
### MRI
- Fat components: **high T1 and T2 signal**, suppression on fat-saturated sequences.
- Soft tissue strands: intermediate signal, enhance after contrast.
- Confirms fatty composition when CT is ambiguous.

## Differentials
 
| Entity                                 | Distinguishing features                                                 |
| -------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Mediastinal lipoma                     | Pure fat, no internal soft tissue elements                              |
| [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Multisystem/Neoplasm/Solid malignancy/Germ cell tumors/Teratoma\|Teratoma]]                           | Fat **plus** calcification, fluid, and other elements (multi-tissue)    |
| Pericardial fat pad                    | Symmetric, no soft tissue strands, typical cardiophrenic location       |
| Morgagni hernia                        | Right anterior cardiophrenic, contiguous with intra-abdominal fat/bowel |
| [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Chest/Mediastinum/Thymic rebound\|Thymic hyperplasia]] | Preserved thymic shape, soft tissue >> fat, drops on opposed-phase MRI  |

## Management
- **Surgical resection** (thymectomy/thymolipomectomy) is definitive and curative.
- Recurrence is rare; no role for adjuvant therapy.
---
 
> [!tip] Exam pearls
> - **Pseudocardiomegaly on CXR + fat-containing anterior mediastinal mass on CT** is the classic vignette.
> - **Fat + soft tissue strands contiguous with thymus** is essentially diagnostic; lipoma is pure fat without strands.
> - Despite huge size, behaviour is benign and non-invasive.
> - Trace continuity with the diaphragm/abdomen to exclude **Morgagni hernia** before committing to the diagnosis.
