> [!summary]
> - A malignant, highly cellular **embryonal tumour of the cerebellum (CNS WHO grade 4)**. 
> - The classic study is a **midline posterior-fossa mass in a child** that is hyperdense on CT and shows **marked diffusion restriction**, often with obstructive hydrocephalus. 
> - It spreads through CSF, so staging requires the **entire neuraxis**. 
> - In adults it is more often **lateral in a cerebellar hemisphere**. 
> - Imaging may suggest a molecular group, but definitive classification requires tissue and molecular testing.

## Exam points
- Most common malignant brain tumour of childhood; peak presentation is approximately **3–8 years**, but it occurs in infants and adults.
- **Child:** usually midline vermis/fourth-ventricle region.
- **Adult:** more often a lateral cerebellar-hemisphere mass, frequently SHH-activated.
- Hallmark MRI feature: **high DWI signal with low ADC** due to hypercellularity.
- Major staging concern: **leptomeningeal and drop metastases**.

---
## WHO 2021 integrated classification

The molecular diagnosis is more important than the historical label “PNET.” Current principal types are:

| Molecular type | Typical clues | Broad clinical tendency |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **WNT-activated** | CTNNB1 pathway; sometimes germline **APC**; may arise near a cerebellar peduncle/CPA | Uncommon; generally very favourable outcome in children |
| **SHH-activated, TP53-wildtype** | Infants and adults; often lateral cerebellar hemisphere | Heterogeneous outcome |
| **SHH-activated, TP53-mutant** | Older children; consider germline **TP53** | High-risk biology |
| **Non-WNT/non-SHH** | Encompasses **group 3 and group 4**; usually midline | Group 3 is often metastatic/MYC-driven and higher risk; group 4 is the most common group |

Group 3 and group 4 remain clinically useful terms, but reliable separation generally requires **DNA-methylation profiling**. Imaging phenotype can raise a suggestion only.

## Imaging

### CT
- Usually **hyperattenuating** relative to cerebellar parenchyma because of high cellularity.
- Midline lesion may efface/compress the fourth ventricle and cause **obstructive hydrocephalus** with transependymal CSF flow.
- Enhancement is variable and often heterogeneous.
- Small calcific or haemorrhagic foci can occur, but prominent calcification, haemorrhage or cystic change should broaden the differential.

### MRI
- **T1:** hypo- to isointense.
- **T2/FLAIR:** variable; the solid tumour is often relatively iso-/hypointense for a paediatric tumour because of high cellularity.
- **DWI/ADC:** typically **marked restricted diffusion**.
- **Post-contrast:** variable, from minimal/patchy to avid heterogeneous enhancement; lack of strong enhancement does not exclude medulloblastoma.
- **SWI/GRE:** assess haemorrhage and calcification.
- **MRS:** high choline, low NAA and a **taurine peak near 3.35 ppm** may support an embryonal tumour but are not required for diagnosis.

### Location and molecular-group clues

| Imaging location | Group suggested |
| --- | --- |
| Cerebellar peduncle, lateral recess or CPA-region tumour | **WNT** |
| Lateral cerebellar hemisphere | **SHH** |
| Midline/vermis with fourth-ventricle involvement | **Group 3 or group 4** |

These are tendencies with substantial overlap—**do not assign a molecular group from MRI alone**.

### Dissemination
- Leptomeningeal spread may be **linear or nodular** along:
	- Cerebral/cerebellar sulci and basal cisterns
	- Ependymal surfaces
	- Spinal cord and cauda equina (“drop metastases”)
- Deposits can show **diffusion restriction with little or no enhancement**; inspect DWI as well as post-contrast images.
- MRI and CSF cytology are complementary: one may be positive when the other is negative.

## Differential diagnosis

| Entity | Distinguishing clues |
| --- | --- |
| **[[Ependymoma\|Ependymoma]]** | Fourth-ventricle **floor-based**, moulds through the foramina of Luschka/Magendie; more heterogeneous with calcification/cysts; usually less marked restriction |
| **[[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Neuro/Brain/CNS Neoplasm/Pilocytic astrocytoma\|Pilocytic astrocytoma]]** | Cyst with enhancing mural nodule or predominantly solid mass; usually high T2 signal and **facilitated diffusion/high ADC** |
| **[[Atypical teratoid-rhabdoid tumour\|Atypical teratoid-rhabdoid tumour]]** | Usually **<3 years**; often eccentric and very heterogeneous with necrosis/haemorrhage; can also restrict diffusion |
| **Exophytic brainstem glioma** | Epicentre in and expansion of the pons/medulla rather than the cerebellum |
| **Choroid plexus tumour** | Frond-like, very avidly enhancing intraventricular mass; hydrocephalus may be disproportionate |

In an adult with a lateral cerebellar mass, also consider **metastasis**; medulloblastoma is uncommon but important, particularly when the lesion is markedly diffusion-restricting.

> [!warning] Common traps
> - Not every medulloblastoma enhances avidly.
> - A fourth-ventricle mass that exits through the foramina is more typical of ependymoma, but location alone is not absolute.
> - Restricted diffusion strongly supports a hypercellular tumour but is **not specific**; ATRT and lymphoma may also restrict.
> - Do not overlook small non-enhancing metastatic deposits on DWI.

## Tumour-predisposition associations
- **WNT:** [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/GI/GI tract/Familial adenomatous polyposis\|Familial adenomatous polyposis]]/[[Turcot syndrome\|Turcot syndrome]] from germline **APC** alteration.
- **SHH:** [[Gorlin syndrome\|Gorlin syndrome]] (**PTCH1/SUFU**) and Li-Fraumeni syndrome (**TP53**).
