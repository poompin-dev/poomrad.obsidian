> [!info] Key points
> - Most common neonatal GI emergency; predominantly preterm (<32/40, <1500 g VLBW)
> - **Pneumatosis intestinalis** is pathognomonic; **portal venous gas (PVG)** is ominous but no longer an absolute surgical indication
> - Terminal ileum and right colon most commonly affected (watershed)
> - AXR is first-line; US is increasingly used and more sensitive for PVG and bowel wall perfusion
> - Only absolute surgical indication is **pneumoperitoneum**

---
## Epidemiology & risk factors
- 5-10% of VLBW infants; inverse relationship with gestational age and birth weight
- Onset typically week 2-3 of life (later in more preterm infants)
- **Term infants**: think congenital heart disease (HLHS, coarctation), polycythaemia, perinatal asphyxia, sepsis, gastroschisis
- Other risks: formula feeding, rapid feed advancement, indomethacin, umbilical catheters, hypotension

## Pathophysiology
Multifactorial: mucosal ischaemia + bacterial colonisation + enteral substrate. Mucosal injury allows bacterial translocation into bowel wall, gas-forming organisms produce H₂ leading to pneumatosis, gas tracks into mesenteric/portal veins, transmural necrosis and perforation follow.

## Clinical features
Feeding intolerance, bilious aspirates, abdominal distension, bloody stools, temperature instability, lethargy, apnoea, metabolic acidosis, thrombocytopenia, DIC in advanced disease.

**Modified Bell staging**
- **I**: suspected (clinical only, non-specific AXR)
- **IIA**: definite, mild (pneumatosis)
- **IIB**: definite, moderate (PVG ± ascites)
- **IIIA**: advanced, bowel intact
- **IIIB**: advanced, perforated

## Imaging

### AXR (supine AP ± left lateral decubitus or cross-table lateral)

|Sign|Significance|
|---|---|
|Bowel dilatation, disordered gas pattern|Early, non-specific|
|**Fixed/persistent loop** (same loop on serial films ≥24h)|Necrotic/ischaemic segment until proven otherwise|
|**Pneumatosis intestinalis**|Pathognomonic. Bubbly (submucosal) vs linear/curvilinear (subserosal, more advanced)|
|**Portal venous gas**|Branching lucencies extending to liver **periphery** (cf. pneumobilia, central)|
|Featureless gasless abdomen|Ominous; fluid-filled bowel, often pre-perforation|
|Pneumoperitoneum|Surgical emergency|

**Pneumoperitoneum signs on supine AXR**: Rigler, football, falciform ligament, triangle, lucent liver, cupola.
### US
- More sensitive than AXR for PVG (echogenic foci moving in portal vein) and pneumatosis
- Bowel wall thickening early; thinning late (ominous)
- **Colour Doppler**: absent perfusion = non-viable bowel, predicts surgical disease
- Echogenic/complex free fluid suggests perforation
### Serial imaging: signs of resolution

| Finding           | Improving                                      | Pseudo-improvement / worsening                 |
| ----------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------- |
| Pneumatosis       | Clearing within 24-72h                         | Persistent >72h or extending                   |
| PVG               | Resolves within hours (often transient anyway) | Persistent or new                              |
| Bowel gas pattern | Returning to normal calibre                    | Becoming **gasless** (NOT reassurance)         |
| Fixed loop        | Same loop now mobile on serial films           | Persistent ≥24-36h or new in different segment |
| Bowel wall on US  | Perfusion returning on colour Doppler          | Mural thinning, absent perfusion               |
| Pneumoperitoneum  | Absent throughout                              | Any new free gas = surgery                     |

Typical recovery timeline: PVG clears in hours, pneumatosis in 24-72h, ileus in 3-5 days, feeds restart cautiously about 1 week after pneumatosis clearance.

## Differentials

| Entity                                           | Discriminator                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **[[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/Ped/Ped abdomen/Spontaneous intestinal perforation\|Spontaneous intestinal perforation]] (SIP)** | Earlier (<7d), extreme prematurity, indomethacin/postnatal steroids, focal perforation near ileocaecal valve, **no pneumatosis** |
| Hirschsprung-associated enterocolitis            | Older onset, delayed meconium, transition zone on contrast enema                                                                 |
| Malrotation with midgut volvulus                 | Bilious vomiting, whirlpool sign, abnormal SMA/SMV relationship                                                                  |
| Sepsis with ileus                                | Diffuse dilatation, no pneumatosis                                                                                               |
| Milk protein allergy                             | Bloody stools, well infant                                                                                                       |

See also: [[30_Rad Knowledge/302_Ddx/Ped/NEC vs SIP\|NEC vs SIP]]

## Management
- **Bell I-II**: NPO, NG decompression, broad-spectrum IV antibiotics, TPN, serial AXR/US every 6-12h
- **Bell III / pneumoperitoneum**: surgical consult. Options are **primary peritoneal drainage** (smaller/sicker infants) vs **laparotomy with resection ± stoma**
- **Surgical indications**: pneumoperitoneum (absolute), clinical deterioration, fixed loop, abdominal wall erythema, palpable mass, persistent thrombocytopenia/acidosis. PVG is now **relative**, not absolute.

**Complications**: post-NEC strictures (colon > ileum, 10-25%, present weeks-months later with obstruction), short bowel syndrome, adhesions, TPN cholestasis, neurodevelopmental delay.

> [!tip] Exam pearls
> 
> - **Pneumatosis location**: bubbly = submucosal (early); linear/curvilinear = subserosal (advanced)
> - **Persistent loop sign**: same loop on serial films ≥24-36h = necrotic bowel
> - **Football + falciform sign** on supine film = massive pneumoperitoneum, do not wait for decubitus
> - **Term infant with NEC**: look for left-sided obstructive CHD or polycythaemia
> - SIP is the key differential: focal, earlier, no pneumatosis, often near ileocaecal valve

> [!warning] Common traps
> 
> - "Gasless abdomen" is **NOT** improvement; it usually means fluid-filled, atonic, infarcted bowel
> - Decompression of a previously dilated loop may reflect perforation, not recovery
> - Clearing pneumatosis with worsening clinical picture can mean transmural necrosis (loss of gas-producing mucosa)
> - PVG resolution alone is not recovery; it is normally transient
> - New fixed loop in a different location = extending disease, even if original pneumatosis has cleared
> - Absence of pneumatosis does **not** exclude NEC (AXR sensitivity 50-75%)
