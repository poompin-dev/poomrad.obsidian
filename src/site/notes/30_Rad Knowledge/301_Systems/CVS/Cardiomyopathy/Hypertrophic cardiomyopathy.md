> [!info] Key points
> - LV wall thickness ≥15 mm (or ≥13 mm with family history / pathogenic sarcomere variant) not explained by abnormal loading
> - Most common inherited cardiomyopathy; AD; **MYBPC3** and **MYH7** dominate
> - CMR is the reference standard for morphology, fibrosis, and SCD risk stratification
> - LGE ≥15% of LV mass and LV apical aneurysm are independent SCD risk markers
> - Obstructive HCM is treated stepwise: beta-blocker → disopyramide / mavacamten → septal reduction therapy

---
## Epidemiology & genetics
- Prevalence ~1 in 500; most common monogenic cardiac disease
- Autosomal dominant, variable penetrance and expressivity
- Sarcomeric protein mutations: MYBPC3 and MYH7 most common; TNNT2, TNNI3, TPM1 less so
- Phenocopies: Anderson-Fabry, cardiac amyloid, Danon, Noonan, PRKAG2, mitochondrial
## Pathophysiology
- Sarcomere mutation → myocyte disarray, interstitial fibrosis, intramural arteriolar dysplasia (small-vessel ischaemia)
- LVOT obstruction is dynamic: hypertrophied basal septum + anterior displacement of mitral apparatus + elongated leaflets + Venturi/drag forces → **SAM** of mitral valve → mid-systolic septal contact → posteriorly directed MR
- Many phenotypic features (elongated leaflets, anomalous PM insertion, apical aneurysm, small-vessel disease) are **not explained by sarcomere mutation alone**
## Clinical features
- Asymptomatic (incidental murmur, ECG abnormality, family screening) → exertional dyspnoea, angina, pre-syncope, syncope, sudden cardiac death
- Ejection systolic murmur at LLSE, increases with Valsalva / standing (opposite of AS)
- ECG: LVH with strain, deep TWI (apical HCM: giant negative T waves V4-V6), pathological Q waves

## Imaging / investigations

### Echocardiography (first-line)
- Asymmetric septal hypertrophy, septal:posterior wall ratio >1.3
- SAM of mitral valve, LVOT gradient (rest and provoked), posteriorly directed MR jet
- **Exercise stress echo** preferred over Valsalva for provoking gradient (Valsalva misses ~50%)
- LVOT vs MR Doppler: LVOT is **dagger-shaped, <7 m/s, late-peaking**; MR is **bell-shaped, >7 m/s, holosystolic**
- Pitfalls: foreshortening misses apical disease; crista supraventricularis can be mistaken for septum and inflate measurement

### CMR (reference standard)
- Cine SSFP: wall thickness measured end-diastole, short-axis, perpendicular to septum, segment of greatest thickness
- Identifies focal/segmental hypertrophy missed on echo (anterolateral free wall, posterior septum, apex)
- Detects **apical aneurysm** (often missed on echo) and **mid-cavity hourglass obstruction**
- **LGE**: patchy mid-myocardial, in hypertrophied segments, classically at **RV insertion points**; quantified at 6 SD above nulled myocardium
- T1 mapping / ECV: elevated native T1, raised ECV reflecting interstitial fibrosis
- Myocardial crypts: deep blood-filled invaginations, early marker in genotype-positive phenotype-negative carriers

### Morphological subtypes
- Asymmetric septal (~70%, most common)
- Apical (Yamaguchi): ace-of-spades LV cavity, giant negative T waves, more common in East Asian populations
- Concentric (mimics amyloid / hypertensive heart)
- Mid-ventricular: predisposes to apical aneurysm
- Mass-like / focal: can mimic tumour, isointense on all sequences

## Differentials of increased LV wall thickness

|Mimic|Discriminator|
|---|---|
|Athlete's heart|Wall ≤13 mm, normal/large cavity, normal diastolic function, no LGE, regresses with detraining|
|Hypertensive heart|Concentric, BP history, regresses with control|
|Cardiac amyloid|Concentric, low ECG voltage, diffuse subendocardial / global LGE, abnormal nulling, very high native T1 and ECV|
|Anderson-Fabry|**Low native T1**, basal inferolateral mid-wall LGE|
|Aortic stenosis|Loading explanation present|
## Management
### Obstructive HCM (LVOT gradient ≥30 mmHg rest or provoked)

- First-line: beta-blocker; non-DHP CCB (verapamil) if BB contraindicated
- Second-line: disopyramide add-on
- **Mavacamten** (cardiac myosin inhibitor) for symptomatic obstructive HCM refractory to first-line
- Refractory: septal reduction therapy
    - Surgical myectomy: younger, complex anatomy, submitral abnormalities, anomalous PM insertion (cannot be ablated)
    - Alcohol septal ablation: older, comorbid, favourable septal perforator anatomy; ~10% need pacemaker
- Avoid: digoxin, nitrates, ACEi, DHP CCB, high-dose diuretics (all worsen obstruction)

### Non-obstructive HCM
- Symptom control; ~10% progress to end-stage with systolic dysfunction → transplant evaluation

### ICD (primary prevention)
- ESC HCM-Risk-SCD score (age, FHx SCD, syncope, max wall thickness, LA diameter, LVOT gradient, NSVT)
- ACC/AHA: risk-marker approach with higher sensitivity than ESC score
- CMR modifiers: **LGE ≥15% LV mass, apical aneurysm, LVEF <50%, wall thickness ≥30 mm**
- Cascade screening of first-degree relatives: ECG + echo every 12-18 months in adolescence, every 3-5 years thereafter

> [!tip] Exam pearls
> 
> - LGE at RV insertion points = expanded extracellular matrix from intersecting myofibrils, **not true scar** → low risk; do not over-call
> - Apical aneurysm carries SCD risk **and** thromboembolic risk → anticoagulation consideration even without AF
> - Septal thickness as low as 10-15 mm can still cause LVOT obstruction if mitral leaflets are elongated or PM anatomy is abnormal
> - Anomalous PM insertion directly into AML (no chordae) → **must go to surgery, not alcohol ablation**
> - Apical HCM is not a benign variant: natural history matches other non-obstructive HCM
> - Low native T1 → Fabry; high T1 with abnormal myocardial nulling → amyloid; patchy mid-wall LGE in hypertrophied segments → HCM
