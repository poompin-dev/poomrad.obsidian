> [!summary]
> **Hereditary form of [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/MSK/Bone/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|hypertrophic osteoarthropathy]]** characterised by **digital clubbing, pachydermia, and bilateral symmetric periostosis**. It usually begins around puberty, has no underlying secondary cause, and may show **exuberant periostosis extending to the epiphyses**.

---

## Terminology and pathophysiology

- **Primary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy (PHO)** = **pachydermoperiostosis**
- Caused by impaired prostaglandin E2 degradation, most often from pathogenic variants in:
	- `HPGD`
	- `SLCO2A1`
- Most genetically confirmed cases are **autosomal recessive**; rare autosomal dominant `SLCO2A1`-related disease has also been described.
- A negative family history does **not** exclude PHO.
- The diagnosis requires exclusion of [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/MSK/Bone/Bony lesions/Sclerotic lesions/Secondary hypertrophic osteoarthropathy\|secondary HOA]], particularly in a patient with new adult-onset disease.

---

## Clinical presentation

- Insidious onset, usually in **childhood or adolescence**, with progression around puberty before eventual stabilisation
- Male predominance and generally more severe expression in males
- **Digital clubbing** with enlargement of the hands and feet
- **Pachydermia**
	- Coarse facial features and deep facial furrows
	- Thickened, folded scalp (**cutis verticis gyrata**)
	- Seborrhoea, hyperhidrosis, and sometimes ptosis
- Arthralgia, joint swelling, effusions, or arthritis may occur; significant joint pain is **not absent by definition**

### Clinical phenotypes

| Form | Manifestations |
| --- | --- |
| **Complete** | Clubbing + periostosis + pachydermia |
| **Incomplete** | Predominant skeletal disease with little or no pachydermia |
| **Forme fruste** | Predominant pachydermia with minimal periostosis |

## Imaging

### Radiograph

- **Bilateral, symmetric periosteal new bone formation**
	- Long bones: radius, ulna, tibia, and fibula are commonly involved
	- Short tubular bones of the hands and feet may also be involved
- Initially affects the **diaphyses and metaphyses**
- With chronic disease:
	- Thick, irregular, or **shaggy** periostosis
	- Cortical thickening and expansion of the tubular bones
	- Extension into the **epiphyses**, sometimes with ill-defined bony excrescences
- Associated soft-tissue enlargement and digital clubbing
- Acro-osteolysis may occasionally occur
- Axial skeletal involvement is uncommon

> [!tip]
> **Epiphyseal extension favours primary HOA over secondary HOA**, but it is not sufficient by itself—clinical exclusion of a secondary cause remains essential.

### Bone scintigraphy

- Sensitive but nonspecific
- Symmetric linear cortical uptake along long bones may produce the **[[70_Glossary/MSK/Double stripe sign\|double-stripe / tramline sign]]**
