> [!summary] Keypoint
> Drugs that **suppress osteoclast-mediated bone resorption**, increase **bone mineral density (BMD)**, and reduce fragility fracture risk, but can rarely cause **low-turnover / suppressed-remodelling skeletal complications**.

---
## Main drugs

### Bisphosphonates
- **Oral:** alendronate, risedronate, ibandronate
- **Intravenous:** zoledronate, pamidronate
- Bind bone mineral → taken up by osteoclasts → osteoclast inhibition/apoptosis
- Long skeletal half-life → effect persists after stopping
### Denosumab
- Monoclonal antibody against **receptor activator of nuclear factor kappa-B ligand (RANKL)**
- Suppresses osteoclast formation and function
- Effect is reversible; stopping abruptly can cause **rebound vertebral fractures**

## Typical indications
- **[[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/MSK/Bone/Metabolic/Osteoporosis/Osteoporosis\|Osteoporosis]]**
- [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/MSK/Bone/Bony lesions/Lytic lesions/Malignant/Multiple myeloma\|Multiple myeloma]]
- Bone metastases
- Hypercalcaemia of malignancy
- Osteogenesis imperfecta, especially paediatric intravenous pamidronate
## Complications
- [[30_Rad Knowledge/301_Systems/MSK/Bone/Trauma/Special fracture/Atypical femoral fracture\|Atypical femoral fracture]]: classically with long-term bisphosphonate therapy, but also possible with other anti-resorptives.
- Medication-related osteonecrosis of the jaw (MRONJ)
	- Associated with bisphosphonates, denosumab, and some antiangiogenic drugs
	- Risk much higher in cancer patients on high-dose antiresorptives
---
## Radiology pearls
- **Metaphyseal sclerotic bands** aka "zebra stripe lines": dense transverse metaphyseal bands parallel to the physis in growing children, classically with cyclical intravenous **pamidronate** for osteogenesis imperfecta.
