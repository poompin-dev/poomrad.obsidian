> [!info] Key points
> - **Pancreatic lipomatosis/fatty replacement** = fatty infiltration or replacement of pancreatic parenchyma; common in adults and often metabolic/age-related.
> - **Lipomatous pseudohypertrophy** = rare/extreme form with **focal or diffuse pancreatic enlargement** due to replacement of pancreatic acinar tissue by mature fat.
> - Ductal anatomy, supporting stroma, and **islets of Langerhans may be relatively preserved**, so exocrine insufficiency may predominate over endocrine failure in true lipomatous pseudohypertrophy.
> - Classic paediatric/young adult associations of fatty pancreatic replacement: **cystic fibrosis**, **Shwachman-Diamond syndrome**, and **Johanson-Blizzard syndrome**.
> - Common adult associations of pancreatic lipomatosis: obesity, type 2 diabetes mellitus, metabolic syndrome, ageing, chronic pancreatitis/duct obstruction, and steroid/Cushing states.
> - MRI is the best problem-solving modality: **fat-suppressed sequences confirm macroscopic fat**, while **in/out-of-phase chemical shift imaging** helps detect microscopic/intravoxel fat and resolve mass mimics.
 
---
## Epidemiology & risk factors
- **Children/young adults:** cystic fibrosis (CFTR mutation), Shwachman-Diamond syndrome, Johanson-Blizzard syndrome.
- **Adults:** obesity, T2DM, metabolic syndrome, ageing, chronic alcohol use, Cushing syndrome/exogenous steroids
## Pathophysiology
Acinar cell loss with adipocyte infiltration. The supporting stroma, ductal system, and islet cells are relatively preserved, so the gland retains its overall shape and appear bulky.
## Clinical features
- Often **incidental** on imaging.
- **Exocrine insufficiency:** steatorrhoea, weight loss, fat-soluble vitamin deficiency (ADEK), malabsorption.
- **Endocrine insufficiency** (DM) is a late feature.
- In Shwachman-Diamond: also bone marrow failure (neutropenia), metaphyseal chondrodysplasia, short stature.

## Imaging

### Ultrasound
- **Hyperechoic pancreas** due to fatty replacement/infiltration.
- Often difficult to distinguish from surrounding retroperitoneal fat and limited by bowel gas and body habitus.
- Gland contour may be preserved; in lipomatous pseudohypertrophy the gland may appear enlarged/bulky.
### CT
- **Low attenuation** of pancreatic parenchyma approaching fat density (-30 to -100 HU depending on degree of replacement).
- Preserved gland contour and lobulated outline; pancreatic duct may be visible threading through fat.
- Residual islands of soft-tissue-attenuation parenchyma may remain (do not mistake for mass).
- No enhancement of fatty regions.
### MRI
- **T1:** high signal if macroscopic fat is present.
- **T2:** variable; fat is often intermediate-to-high signal.
- **Fat-suppressed sequences:** signal suppression confirms macroscopic fat.
- **In/out-of-phase chemical shift imaging:** signal drop helps confirm microscopic/intravoxel fat, especially in focal fatty replacement or equivocal CT findings.
- **MRCP:** main pancreatic duct calibre is usually preserved unless there is an underlying obstructive cause.

## Differentials

| Entity                         | Distinguishing feature                                                              |
| ------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Liposarcoma**                | Rare; heterogeneous, soft-tissue components, enhancement                            |
| **Chronic pancreatitis**       | Calcifications, ductal dilatation/irregularity, parenchymal atrophy ± fat           |
| **PDAC with upstream atrophy** | Focal hypoenhancing mass, abrupt duct cut-off, distal atrophy ± fat                 |
| **Senile atrophy**             | Age-related, smaller gland, less fat replacement                                    |
## Management
- Usually no treatment if incidental and asymptomatic.
- Treat contributory causes where relevant: weight loss, glycaemic control, alcohol cessation, steroid reduction if clinically appropriate.
- **Pancreatic enzyme replacement therapy (PERT)** for symptomatic exocrine pancreatic insufficiency.
- Supplement fat-soluble vitamins **A, D, E, K** if deficient.
- In cystic fibrosis or Shwachman-Diamond syndrome: multidisciplinary care.
- In Shwachman-Diamond syndrome, monitor for bone marrow failure and myelodysplastic syndrome/acute myeloid leukaemia transformation.
- If focal change, duct cut-off, upstream duct dilatation, or unexplained distal atrophy is present, exclude obstructing **pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC)** rather than assuming benign fatty replacement.
---
 
> [!tip] Exam pearls
> - **Out-of-phase signal drop** on MRI is the single most useful sequence for confirming microscopic fat; combine with fat-sat to characterise macroscopic fat.
 
> [!warning] Common pitfalls
> - Mistaking residual islands of normal parenchyma for a hypoenhancing mass (e.g., PDAC). Look for preserved enhancement pattern and absent ductal cut-off.
> - Focal fatty sparing of the **peribiliary region around the CBD and uncinate** can mimic a hypoattenuating mass on CT. MRI chemical shift resolves this.
